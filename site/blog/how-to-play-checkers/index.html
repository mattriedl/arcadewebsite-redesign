How to Play Checkers: Rules, Setup, Kings and Tips (Play Free Online)

By ArcadeWebsite Team · November 13, 2026 · 7 min read

To play checkers, each player sets 12 pieces on the dark squares of the three rows closest to them, and the players take turns moving one piece one square diagonally forward. You capture an opponent's piece by jumping over it into an empty square, and jumping is required whenever you can. Reach the far side of the board and your piece becomes a king that can move backward too; you win when your opponent has no pieces or no legal moves left.

This guide covers standard American checkers, also called English draughts, played on an 8×8 board. The rules below follow the standard English draughts rules, and we checked how our free Checkers game handles each one by playing it. You can play it right on that page, against the computer or a friend, with no download.

Checkers rules at a glance

Rule Detail
Players 2
Board 8×8 squares; you only play on the 32 dark squares
Pieces 12 per player, usually red (or white) and black
First move The darker pieces (black) move first
Normal moves One square diagonally forward
Captures Jump diagonally over an opponent's piece into an empty square
Must you jump? Yes. If a jump is possible, you have to take it
Kings A piece that reaches the far row is crowned and can move backward
How to win Capture all your opponent's pieces or leave them with no legal move

How to set up checkers

Setting up takes under a minute:

  1. Place the board between the two players so each player has a dark square in the corner on their left. That's how the board in our Checkers game is set up too.
  2. Choose colors. One player takes the 12 dark pieces, the other the 12 light ones.
  3. Put your pieces on the dark squares of the three rows nearest you, four pieces per row. That's 12 pieces each, and 24 on the board in total.
  4. Leave the middle two rows empty. That open space is where the first moves happen.

The row closest to you is your "king row." Your opponent wants to reach it, and you want to reach theirs.

How pieces move in checkers

Players take turns, one move per turn. The player with the dark pieces moves first. In our game, if you choose to play red, the computer (black) makes the first move.

An ordinary piece, sometimes called a "man," moves one square diagonally forward to an empty dark square. It can go forward-left or forward-right, but never straight ahead, sideways or backward. Because pieces only move diagonally, they stay on the dark squares the whole game.

How to jump and capture

To capture, jump your piece diagonally over an opponent's piece that's right next to it, landing on the empty square just beyond. The piece you jumped over is removed from the board.

  • You can only jump if the landing square is empty. If something is behind the opponent's piece, there's no jump.
  • Ordinary pieces jump forward only. Only kings can jump backward.
  • Multiple jumps are allowed. If your piece lands where it can jump again, it keeps going in the same turn, and you must finish every jump in the sequence. A double or triple jump can change direction (forward-left, then forward-right) as long as each jump is forward for an ordinary piece.

Do you have to jump in checkers?

Yes. Under the standard rules, jumping is mandatory: if you can capture, you must, even if it walks your piece into a trap. If you have more than one capture available, you can pick which one to make. You don't have to choose the one that takes the most pieces, but once you start a sequence, you have to make every jump in it.

Some families play an old house rule called "huffing," where you may skip a jump but the opponent can then remove the piece that should have jumped. Huffing isn't part of the official rules. Our online game uses the standard rule; its instructions say, "If you can jump an opponent you must do so."

Checkers king rules

When one of your pieces reaches the far row (your opponent's king row), it's crowned a king. On a real board, you mark it by stacking a second checker of your color on top.

  • Kings move and jump diagonally in any direction, forward or backward, but still only one square at a time (or one jump at a time).
  • Crowning ends your turn. If a piece reaches the king row in the middle of a jump sequence, it stops there and becomes a king. It can't keep jumping backward until your next turn.
  • Kings can be captured like any other piece.

Kings are powerful because they can chase pieces from behind and escape traps, so getting the first king is often a big step toward winning.

How to win at checkers

You win when your opponent can't move on their turn. That happens in two ways:

  1. You capture all of their pieces.
  2. You block them. If every piece they have left is stuck, with no legal move or jump, they lose.

A game is a draw if neither player can force a win, for example when each side has one king left, or if both players agree to a draw. In 2007, a team of Canadian computer scientists led by Jonathan Schaeffer showed that from the starting position, both players can guarantee a draw with perfect play.

Checkers strategy: tips for beginners

You'll win more games with a few simple habits:

  • Look for forced jumps before every move. Since jumping is required, you can offer one piece to pull an opponent's piece into a spot where you then jump two. Watch for your opponent setting the same trap.
  • Keep your back row filled as long as you can. Pieces on your king row stop the other player from crowning. A common approach is to keep two of those pieces home until late in the game.
  • Control the middle. Pieces in the center have more moves and are harder to trap than pieces on the edges.
  • Move in groups. A piece with a friendly piece behind it can't be jumped from the front, because there's no empty landing square.
  • Trade pieces when you're ahead. If you have more pieces, even swaps make your lead count for more.
  • Race for the first king. A king can attack from behind, where ordinary pieces can't defend.

Start on Easy against the computer to practice spotting jumps, then switch to Hard.

Frequently asked questions

How do you set up a checkers board?

Place the board so each player has a dark square in the corner on their left. Each player puts 12 pieces on the dark squares of the three rows nearest them, leaving the two middle rows empty.

Who goes first in checkers?

The player with the dark (black) pieces moves first. After that, players take turns making one move each.

Can you jump backwards in checkers?

Only with a king. Ordinary pieces move and jump diagonally forward only, while kings can move and jump forward or backward.

Do you have to jump in checkers?

Yes. In standard checkers, if you can capture a piece, you must. If you have more than one jump available, you can choose which one to take.

Can a king move more than one square in checkers?

No. In standard American checkers, a king moves one square diagonally in any direction, though it can make several jumps in one turn. "Flying kings" that slide across the board are from other versions, such as international draughts.
