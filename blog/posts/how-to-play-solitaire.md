To play solitaire, deal seven piles of cards with the top card of each pile face up, then move cards to build four piles, one for each suit, from ace up to king. In the seven piles you stack cards in falling order with alternating colors (a red 6 on a black 7), and you turn over cards from the deck when you run out of moves. You win when all 52 cards are on the four suit piles.

This guide covers Klondike, the version most people mean when they say "solitaire" and the one that came with Windows. ## The parts of a solitaire game

The table has four areas:

- **Tableau:** the seven piles across the middle, where most of the game happens.
- **Foundations:** four spaces, usually at the top right, one for each suit.
- **Stock:** the face-down pile of leftover cards, usually at the top left.
- **Waste:** where cards from the stock land. Its top card is in play.

## How to set up solitaire

Here's the standard deal with a real deck:

1. Shuffle a 52-card deck. Take out any jokers.
2. Deal one card face up. That's the first pile.
3. To the right of it, deal one card face down onto each of the next six spots, so you have seven piles in a row.
4. Go back to the second pile and deal one card face up on it, then one card face down on each pile to its right.
5. Keep going the same way. Each round starts one pile further right with a face-up card.
6. When you're done, the first pile has 1 card and the seventh has 7. Each pile has exactly one face-up card on top. That's 28 cards in the tableau.
7. Put the other 24 cards face down above the tableau as the stock. Leave room for four foundations next to it.

## Solitaire rules: how to move cards

These are the rules of solitaire you'll use on every turn.

### Building the foundations

- Each foundation starts with an **ace**. As soon as an ace is free, move it up to an empty foundation.
- After the ace, add cards of the **same suit in order**: 2, 3, 4 and so on up to the king.
- You can move a card to a foundation from the top of a tableau pile or from the top of the waste.

### Building in the tableau

- You can place a card on a tableau pile if it's **one rank lower and the opposite color**. A black 8 can go on a red 9. A red jack can go on a black queen.
- You can move a **group of face-up cards** together, as long as they're already in order. Move the whole run by its top card. For example, a black 9, red 8 and black 7 can move together onto a red 10.
- When you move the last face-up card off a pile, **turn over the face-down card** under it. It's now in play.
- An **empty pile** can only be filled with a **king**, or with a run of cards that starts with a king. No other card can go there.

### Using the stock

- When you can't (or don't want to) move anything else, turn over a card from the stock onto the waste.
- The top card of the waste can be played onto the tableau or a foundation.
- When the stock runs out, flip the waste pile over to make a new stock and go through it again.

Versions differ here. Classic Klondike turns **one card at a time**; harder "draw three" turns three, and only the top one can be played. Some rule sets also limit how many times you can go through the stock. Our Solitaire game turns over one card each time you click the deck.

## How you win (and lose)

You win when all four foundations are complete, from ace to king, with all 52 cards on them. You lose when no useful moves are left. Some deals can't be won however well you play, so don't feel bad. Just deal again.

## Solitaire tips: how to win more often

Solitaire is part luck, but good habits help:

- **Free the face-down cards first.** When you have a choice, make the move that turns over a hidden card, especially in the tall piles on the right.
- **Don't rush cards to the foundations.** Aces and 2s can go up right away, but a 6 or 7 may be needed in the tableau as a home for a 5 or 6 of the other color.
- **Only empty a pile when you have a king ready.** An empty space can only take a king, so clearing one with no king ready gains you nothing.
- **Choose your kings carefully.** With one empty space and two kings, pick the king whose color fits the queens that are waiting.
- **Play the tableau before the stock.** Check every move on the table before turning a new card from the stock.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you set up solitaire?

Deal seven piles in a row: the first pile gets 1 card, the second 2, and so on up to 7 in the last pile. Only the top card of each pile is face up. The other 24 cards go face down as the stock.

### What are the basic rules of solitaire?

Build four foundation piles by suit from ace to king. In the seven tableau piles, stack cards in falling order with alternating colors, turn over face-down cards as you uncover them, and fill empty piles only with kings. Turn cards from the stock when you need new ones.

### Can you put any card in an empty space in solitaire?

No. In classic Klondike solitaire, only a king, or a run of cards that starts with a king, can go in an empty tableau space.

### Is solitaire draw 1 or draw 3?

Both are standard. Draw 1 turns one card from the stock at a time and is easier. Draw 3 turns three at once, and only the top card can be played, which makes it harder. Our online Solitaire game turns one card at a time.

### Is every game of solitaire winnable?

No. Some deals can't be won no matter how you play, because the cards you need stay buried. Even strong players lose plenty of games, so if you're stuck with no moves, just start a new deal.
