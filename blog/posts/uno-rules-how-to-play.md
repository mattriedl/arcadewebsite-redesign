In Uno, every player starts with 7 cards, and on your turn you play one card that matches the top card of the discard pile by color, number or symbol. If you can't play, you draw a card. Shout "Uno!" when you're down to one card, and the first player to get rid of all their cards wins the round.

This guide explains the official Uno rules from Mattel, the company that makes Uno, plus the common house rules that aren't official (like stacking Draw 2s). We checked the rules against Mattel's instruction sheet and well-known rules guides, and we played the free [Uno game](/game/uno/) on our site to see how it works online. ## What's in an Uno deck

The classic deck has 108 cards in four colors: red, yellow, green and blue.

- **Number cards (76):** each color has 19, numbered 0 to 9.
- **Action cards (24):** 8 Skip, 8 Reverse and 8 Draw Two, two of each per color.
- **Wild cards (8):** 4 Wild and 4 Wild Draw Four. These have no color.

Newer decks have 112 cards. The extra ones are special wild cards, such as Wild Shuffle Hands and blank customizable wilds you can write your own rule on. You can play the classic game without them.

## How to set up Uno

1. Each player draws a card. The player with the highest number deals.
2. Shuffle and deal **7 cards** to each player, face down.
3. Put the rest of the deck face down in the middle. This is the draw pile.
4. Turn over the top card of the draw pile to start the discard pile next to it.
5. The player to the dealer's left goes first, and play moves to the left (clockwise).

If the first card turned over is an action card, its action applies to the first player. If it's a Wild, the player to the dealer's left picks the starting color. If it's a Wild Draw Four, put it back in the deck, shuffle and turn over a new card.

## How to play Uno: taking a turn

On your turn, play **one card** from your hand onto the discard pile. It has to match the top card in one of three ways:

- **By color:** a red 3 can go on any red card.
- **By number:** a blue 7 can go on a green 7.
- **By symbol:** a yellow Skip can go on a red Skip.

You can also play a Wild card on anything.

If you have no card you can play, draw one card from the draw pile. If that card can be played, you may play it right away. If not, your turn ends. You're also allowed to draw instead of playing a card you could play, with the same rule for the card you draw.

When the draw pile runs out and nobody has won yet, shuffle the discard pile (except its top card) to make a new draw pile, and keep playing.

## What each special card does

### Skip

The next player loses their turn.

### Reverse

The direction of play switches. If play was going left, it now goes right, and the other way around.

### Draw Two

The next player draws 2 cards and loses their turn. They can't play a card on that turn.

### Wild

You choose the color that play continues in. You can play a Wild at any time, even if you have other cards you could play.

### Wild Draw Four

You choose the next color, and the next player draws 4 cards and loses their turn. The catch: you may only play it when you have **no card that matches the color** of the top card. Holding a card that matches only by number or symbol doesn't count against you.

## Uno Draw 4 rules: how a challenge works

If someone plays a Wild Draw Four on you and you think they had a card of the matching color, you can challenge them. They have to show you their hand.

- **If they played it illegally,** they draw the 4 cards instead of you.
- **If they played it legally,** you lose the challenge and draw 6 cards: the 4 cards plus 2 more as a penalty.

So only challenge when you're fairly sure. A failed challenge costs you two extra cards.

## Calling "Uno"

When you play your second-to-last card, you must say "Uno!" to warn everyone you have one card left. If another player catches you before the next player starts their turn, you draw 2 cards. If nobody catches you in time, you get away with it.

You have to say it every time you get down to one card, not just once per round.

## Uno stacking rules: can you stack Draw 2 and Draw 4?

**No, not in the official rules.** When someone plays a Draw Two or Wild Draw Four on you, you draw the cards and lose your turn. You can't add your own Draw Two to pass a bigger penalty to the next player. Uno's official social media accounts have confirmed this, and lots of fans were surprised.

Stacking is still one of the most popular house rules, and it's fine to use if everyone agrees before you start. Other common house rules include:

- **Draw until you can play:** keep drawing until you get a playable card, instead of drawing just one.
- **Jump-in:** play an identical card (same color and number) out of turn.
- **7-0 rule:** a 7 lets you swap hands with someone, and a 0 makes everyone pass their hand to the next player.

None of these are official, so agree on them first to avoid arguments.

## Uno rules for 2 players

Two-player Uno uses the same rules with two changes:

- **Reverse works like a Skip.** Since there's no one else to pass to, you take another turn right away.
- **When you play a Skip,** you also get to play again immediately.

A Draw Two or Wild Draw Four still makes the other player draw, and then it's your turn again.

## How to win and keep score

A round ends when one player plays their last card. If that last card is a Draw Two or Wild Draw Four, the next player still draws the cards, and they count toward the score.

In the official scoring, the winner of the round gets points for every card left in the other players' hands:

| Card | Points |
|---|---|
| Number cards (0–9) | Face value |
| Draw Two, Reverse, Skip | 20 each |
| Wild, Wild Draw Four (and other special wilds) | 50 each |

The first player to reach **500 points** wins the game. Mattel also gives another way to score: each player adds up the cards left in their own hand after every round, anyone who reaches 500 is out, and the last player still in wins. Plenty of families just play single rounds, where whoever goes out first wins.

## Tips to win more Uno games

- **Save Wild cards for when you need them.** A Wild can get you out of a color you have no cards for, so don't use it early if a normal card will do.
- **Change to the color you have the most of.** When you play a Wild, pick the color that makes up most of your hand.
- **Watch the player with the fewest cards.** Use Skip, Draw Two and Reverse to slow down whoever is about to win.
- **Get rid of high-value cards early.** If someone else goes out, the action cards and wilds left in your hand are worth the most points.
- **Don't forget to call Uno.** Two extra cards at the end of a round is an easy way to lose.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many cards do you start with in Uno?

Each player starts with 7 cards. The rest of the deck becomes the draw pile, and its top card is turned over to start the discard pile.

### Can you stack Draw 2 cards in Uno?

Not in the official rules. If someone plays a Draw Two on you, you draw 2 cards and lose your turn. Stacking is a popular house rule you can use if everyone agrees first.

### Can you play a Wild Draw 4 anytime?

No. You can only play a Wild Draw Four when you have no card matching the color of the top card. If you play it anyway, another player can challenge you, and if you're caught you draw the 4 cards yourself.

### What happens if you forget to say Uno?

If another player catches you with one card before the next player starts their turn, you draw 2 cards. If nobody notices in time, there's no penalty.

### Can you end Uno on a Wild or action card?

Yes. You can go out on any card, including a Wild or an action card. If your last card is a Draw Two or Wild Draw Four, the next player still draws those cards, and they count toward your score.
